# Yami's rulings

Each ruling is a rating Yami decided in review, with the facts it rests on and when it applies again. The skill checks every rating against these before writing it, and cites the ruling it follows ("per R3").

A ruling that generalizes also changes `rating-prompt.md`; the ruling stays here as the worked example. Add new rulings at the end with the next number, and never renumber.

## R1. A target audience enforced by admission counts as the cohort

- **Program:** MIT TLP (`mit-tlp`), executive. **Need:** senior_network. **Rating:** 5 (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** "Relevant roles include: C-level positions including CEOs, CTOs, COOs, CIOs, Chief Architects, etc."; an application with a USD 200 fee; "3 residential weeks". No published median.
- **Why:** admission is by application, so the school enforces the target audience. Yami's own cohort averaged about 22 years of experience. This first-hand knowledge backs the ruling, but never appears in a card note.
- **Applies when:** no median is published, but a target audience at a rubric level and admission by application are. Still list the need under lowEvidence.

## R2. The latest class profile counts, even for an earlier class

- **Program:** Wharton EMBA SF (`wharton-emba-sf`), emba. **Need:** senior_network. **Rating:** 3 (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** Class of 2028 profile, "an average of 13-14 years of professional experience", while admissions describe the 2027 intake.
- **Why:** the most recent published profile is the best evidence of who enrolls, and decision B1 already stores it. An average counts when no median is published, and a range uses its midpoint (B2: 13.5 → band 10 to 14 → 3).
- **Applies when:** the class profile describes a different class from the intake being researched.

## R3. One required leadership course is a 4, not a 5

- **Program:** Northwestern MEM (`northwestern-mem-pt`), specialized_masters. **Need:** leadership_skills. **Rating:** 4 (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** required "MEM 424: Leadership and Organizational Behavior"; rotating leadership roles in a simulation; "leadership is core component of our coursework".
- **Why:** required leadership courses make leadership a major strand, but the curriculum isn't built for experienced leaders. A marketing line that says leadership is "core" doesn't make a 5 on its own.
- **Applies when:** leadership is required and named, but the program is about another field.

## R4. An unlabelled experience figure still sets the band

- **Program:** Northwestern MEM (`northwestern-mem-pt`), specialized_masters. **Need:** senior_network. **Rating:** 2 (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** the homepage says "seven years STEM experience", not labelled as a median, an average or a part-time figure.
- **Why:** it is the only figure the school publishes, and it falls in the 5 to 9 band. Use it, and list the need under lowEvidence.
- **Applies when:** the school states a typical experience figure without saying what kind of figure it is.

## R5. An alumni group alone is a network, not career services

- **Program:** MIT TLP (`mit-tlp`), executive. **Need:** new_industry_or_city. **Rating:** 2 (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** "expand your professional network by joining the global MIT Professional Education Alumni LinkedIn Group"; no career services in the research.
- **Why:** rubric level 2. A network is not support for changing industry, role or city.
- **Applies when:** the only career-related fact is an alumni or professional network.

## R6. No career facts at all: the default or 3, whichever is lower

- **Program:** Wharton EMBA SF (`wharton-emba-sf`), emba. **Need:** new_industry_or_city. **Rating:** 3 (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** none on career services; only "required to maintain full-time employment throughout the duration of the program".
- **Why:** evidence rule for missing facts; list the need under lowEvidence.
- **Open question:** a program with no career facts (3 here) can outrank one whose research shows only a network (2, R5). The fix is more research on the program, not a rating change.

## R7. A degree 4 needs every course to count

- **Program:** Harvard Extension Organizational Behavior certificate (`harvard-ext-org-behavior`), certificate. **Need:** graduate_degree. **Rating:** 3 (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** "you can earn this graduate certificate along the way by completing courses that fulfill both certificate and degree requirements", for the management and industrial-organizational psychology master's; not every certificate course counts.
- **Why:** level 4 needs all credits to count toward a named degree. Here only courses that fulfil both requirements count.
- **Applies when:** a certificate's credits count toward a degree only for some courses.

## R8. Card notes state facts, not judgments

- **Program:** Wharton EMBA SF (`wharton-emba-sf`). **Need:** deep_expertise note (Oct 9).
- **Facts:** "You must complete 19 course units at Wharton to earn your degree from our program."
- **Why:** "broad rather than deep in one field" was an inference, not a fact from the research. The note became "A general MBA of 19 course units."
- **Applies when:** writing any `ratingNotes` line. Judgments go in `reasoning`.
